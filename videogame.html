The digital version.

Same game. Web-native. Built in the open.

JOURNEYWAYS is being built as a digital game in parallel with the board game. Same design philosophy, same five card categories, same modular map, same private journal. Different medium, same commitment to identity exploration and collaborative narrative.

Premise

Premise

The digital version exists to remove barriers, not to replace the table. Players can participate from anywhere; sessions span geographies; there is no camera support so the focus stays on words and stories. Solo and group play, real-time multiplayer, trilingual at launch.

The repository is private during development; access by request. It will be made public when the project is released.

Built

Built so far

Shipped in the current alpha build.

Real-time multiplayer

Lobby, dashboard, and play view show who is present. Collaborative canvas synchronization keeps everyone's view in step.

Five-color card system

The same map-tile and card-pile system as the board game, recreated as an interactive canvas. Red Encounters, Green Movement, Blue Reflections, Black Countdown, Purple Group actions.

Private journal with sketch preview

Per-player journal: write entries, sketch with the canvas tool, browse previous entries with thumbnail previews.

Character origins as PDF

Players can export their character creation page as a US Letter PDF for printing or archival.

Trilingual interface

English, Spanish, and French at launch. User-created content stays in the language the user wrote it; nothing gets auto-translated.

Privacy by design

No camera support. Argon2id password hashing, Cloudflare Turnstile verification, journals scoped per session. All data hosted in Canada.

Recent

Recent milestones

Latest changes in the development branch.

  1. May 2026

    Researchers, invitations, and scheduled rooms

    Research rooms grow up. The person who creates one is its researcher, and they can name other people in the room as researchers too; everyone sees a small tag next to the names so it is clear who is who. A researcher can configure a short set of optional questions (for example an age range, gender identity in your own words, or a country), which you see when you join and can skip; once anyone has answered, the questions are locked so they cannot change underneath you. Joining a research room now names its researchers up front and asks you to join only if you know and trust them. Room creators can also invite people by email, and open a room on a schedule so it stays closed until a chosen time.

  2. May 2026

    Research rooms and open play

    When you create a room you now choose whether it is for research or for open play. Joining a research room shows a short, plain-language screen explaining that the content you create there may be quoted, always anonymised, in the project's research, and joining is how you agree to that. Open play rooms stay private to the people in them. The choice is shown clearly on the room and on your dashboard.

  3. May 2026

    Policy versions, recorded consent

    Terms of Service and Privacy Policy now show their version and effective date at the top. When we update them in a way that changes what you agreed to, the next time you sign in you'll see a short re-acceptance screen with links to the updated text. Each acceptance is recorded against your account, so the record of what you agreed to, and when, stays with you.

  4. May 2026

    Live multiplayer presence

    The "online" badge next to each room on your dashboard now updates in real time as players come and go, no page refresh needed. The lobby chips and the in-room presence pill also got a tighter visual pass with a "you" marker so it is clear which traveller is yours.

  5. May 2026

    Right to forget your account

    A new Danger zone on your profile lets you delete your account yourself, with a 30-day grace window so you can change your mind. Four reminder emails arrive over the month, each with a one-click cancel link, plus a confirmation when the deletion completes. If you created rooms where other players have written journals, the action is blocked until you handle those rooms first, so collaborators don't lose work without notice. Available in English, French, and Spanish.

  6. May 2026

    Privacy audit pass

    Stopped logging usernames at signup, removed an unused third-party analytics entry from the Content Security Policy, and verified the live deployment matches the privacy policy. Behind-the-scenes work.

  7. May 2026

    Security and reliability audit

    Closed several account-enumeration and brute-force vectors, made email verification and password reset race-safe, and cut admin-page query times via new indexes and query rewrites. Mostly behind-the-scenes work.

  8. May 2026

    Password reset works

    The "forgot password?" email now lands on a working form. Set a new password and sign back in.

  9. May 2026

    Brand-aligned visual refresh

    The videogame UI now follows the canonical palette (gray-900 base, yellow-400 accent), self-hosts Inter and Italianno, and uses script titles on atmospheric page headers. High-contrast checkboxes and toggles, with green and red reserved for semantic states.

Roadmap

Roadmap

Planned, not yet shipped.

  1. Story loop

    The first end-to-end journaling turn: pick a card from a chosen suit, write a short entry, share it with the room.

  2. In-room chat

    Plain text chat inside your room, alongside the canvas and the journal. Voice and video stay outside the application; bring your own.

  3. Take your stories with you

    Export your character data, narrative entries, and reflections in a portable format you can keep, even after the session ends.

  4. Mobile responsiveness

    Phones are advisory for now. Full small-screen support is planned.

  5. Open documentation

    The full project, in plain technical writing, visible on the site: security, privacy, data governance, infrastructure. For anyone who wants to look under the hood.

  6. Quiet beta, then larger sessions

    First closed beta with a small group, then larger play sessions. No launch campaign; updates here as they happen.

  7. Hybrid play (longer-term)

    A mixed-medium variant that combines physical components, like the player booklet, with digital ones, like the board map.
