JOURNEYWAYS

A board game and a digital game for the elaboration of identity beyond colonial categories.

5-minute presentation · Adri M. · journeyways.ca

Cover · ~20s

  • Open with the project name and the one-line frame. Do not over-explain.
  • Anchor: master’s research at UBC GRSJ. Academic context, signaled briefly.
  • Promise: ten slides, five minutes, friendly tone.
The problem

Identity research begins with a form.

Most studies of gender identity start with a handful of boxes (transman, transwoman, ciswoman, cisman, nonbinary, other) and a semi-structured interview to fit a person inside them.

The categories are eurocentric. The interviews are sterile. The setup is built for extraction, not elaboration.

The problem · ~30s

  • Name the standard method: a form plus a semi-structured interview.
  • Name the critique: categories are eurocentric, the format is sterile, the structure compresses what it claims to study.
  • This is the gap the project responds to. Do not linger.
The proposition

What if the research happened around a table, not across one?

JOURNEYWAYS is a board game and a digital game built as research instruments. Identity here is whatever the player decides it to be. As large or small, real or imagined, expressed in writing, drawing, speaking, or silence.

The goal is to make a space where people can elaborate their own stories without being categorized, judged, or hurried.

“It is a game. We are just playing. Everything is valid. You can’t lose.”

The proposition · ~35s

  • Reframe the move: research as a table, not a clipboard.
  • Identity is what the player decides it to be. Five modalities (write, draw, speak, doodle, silent).
  • The pull-quote is the emotional anchor of the whole talk. Read it slowly.
Three components

Map tiles, cards, and a player booklet.

Map tiles

Wordless illustrations. Place them, draw on them, expand them. The map is open to interpretation and to authorship.

Cards

Short text, public to the table, organized in five colored piles. Stimulus, not script.

Player booklet

Write, draw, doodle, comic, or do nothing. Every mode counts. The booklet leaves a durable archive.

Rules-light · Open to authorship · Modifiable in play

Three components · ~30s

  • Name the canonical triad. These three together identify the game.
  • Tiles do location. Cards do stimulus. Booklet does expression and archive.
  • End on the bottom strip: rules-light, open, modifiable. Three principles, no preamble.
Bounded randomness

Five colored piles, not one shuffled deck.

Players choose which kind of moment to invite. The specific card stays a discovery. Agency at the level of category, surprise at the level of content.

Bounded randomness · ~30s

  • Five categories, five colored piles.
  • The point is the design move: agency over kind, discovery over specifics.
  • It lets players steer the tone of the story without collapsing the surprise.
How it’s built

Five principles, of nine.

  • Identity stays in the player’s hands.
  • No winning, no losing.
  • Everyone at the table consents to what happens at the table.
  • Prompts elicit; they do not provide.
  • Simple-yet-sensorial materials. Hand-made wordmark. Freely reproducible.

The full nine, with materials and examples, live on /design.

How it’s built · ~35s

  • Five of the nine principles. Read each one with breath between.
  • Consent is the only non-negotiable rule. Everything else is open to authorship.
  • “Elicit, not provide” is the inversion of standard prompt-driven games.
Stories

From actual playtests.

A player once decided their character to be Water. Not water-based. Not anthropomorphized. Simply Water. As a way to think about feeling less flowy than they used to be.

Another player broke the rule that tiles must be placed adjacent. They placed a space-themed tile separately and authored warp rules to connect it. The break became the best thing about that session.

“The more the players create new rules, the better the game goes.”

Stories · ~40s

  • Both stories are anonymized; both players gave verbal consent. Use them freely.
  • Water shows the breadth of identity allowed. The warp tile shows rule-breaking as success metric.
  • The closing line is the thesis of the design philosophy in fifteen words.
Physical and digital

Two versions, peers, not hierarchy.

Physical

Cardboard and wooden tiles. Drawable, expandable. Played at a table. Sixty to ninety minutes for a group of five or six.

Digital

Real-time, solo or group. English, Spanish, French at launch. No camera support. Cloud-saved journals. Privacy by design.

Same components, same philosophy, same agency. Open source: code under a software license, content under Creative Commons. No siloing in.

Physical and digital · ~35s

  • Both versions are peers. Neither is canonical.
  • Digital exists for access and reach, not because the physical was insufficient.
  • Privacy-by-design is the deliberate move: no camera in the digital version, on purpose.
  • Open source, two licenses, no proprietary forks.
What this is for

A three-phase research arc.

Now

Playtesting refines the instrument. Players are testers, not research subjects. Diverse groups have already played.

2026

Ethics review and formal protocol. Informed consent, audio-only documentation, ethnographic frame.

2026 to 2028

Formal research phase. Ethnographic analysis of the stories that emerge. Papers, talks, and the open framework as the lasting outputs.

The games are not the project. The project is what the stories show us about how people elaborate identity when they are not under scrutiny.

What this is for · ~40s

  • Phase 1: now. Playtesters refine the tool. No formal study yet.
  • Phase 2: 2026 ethics review. Mention this clearly: the formal study has not begun.
  • Phase 3: through 2028. Ethnographic analysis is the methodological core.
  • Closing line is the figure-ground reversal: the games are instruments, not the artifact.
Success

What success looks like.

“The stories are the most important, not the way the players tell them.”

A successful end of project: a better understanding of how people talk about their identities when they don’t feel under scrutiny, judged, or having to comply with absurd categories. Visibility, where there has been silence.
