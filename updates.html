A guest lecture in GRSJ 306

JOURNEYWAYS was brought into UBC's GRSJ 306 as a guest lecture, sharing the game and its approach to identity, play, and pedagogy with students.

Video game

A table that shows its hands

The shared table now feels alive. When you pick a card or tile it rides with your cursor until you set it down, and everyone watches it move, named to whoever is holding it, since nothing here is secret. Tiles can rest on top of one another in neat little piles with a small count, and moving the top one reveals the tile beneath. Along the bottom of the screen a quiet line tells the room what is happening: who is placing a card, moving a tile, or writing in their journal (never what they wrote). Open it and the line unfolds into a history with terse timestamps.

Video game

A new engine under the table

The game now runs on PostgreSQL. The shared table, every journal, and each session's state are stored as rich, nested objects, and PostgreSQL works with that kind of structured data natively, so the game saves and restores a session more quickly and holds it more reliably. Nothing changes in how you play; the engine just rests on sturdier ground.

Video game

A table to play on

The cards and map tiles now sit on a shared table. Everything is shuffled into uneven, face-down piles, each showing its suit on the back and the tiles on their wooden backs. Pick the top of a pile and it turns face up for the whole room to see, then place it wherever it belongs. An organizer can choose how many Countdown cards are in play and reshuffle the deck during a session, with everyone asked to agree first.

Video game

One catalogue, drawn once

Every card and tile is now drawn from a single shared definition, so the catalogue on this site, your dashboard, and the live game table all show exactly the same components. Each one scales cleanly to whatever size it needs while keeping its true proportions: a card stays a poker card, a tile stays a post-it square, and the art and text shrink and grow together.

May 2026
Video game

Cards and map tiles, online

The full deck and the modular map now have a live catalogue you can browse on the site: every card sorted by suit, and every map tile, including the blank tiles you fill in yourself. Each set comes from the game itself, so the website always shows what is actually in play. See the cards and the map tiles.

Video game

Researchers, invitations, and scheduled rooms

Research rooms grow up. The person who creates one is its researcher, and can name others as researchers too, with a small tag beside each name. A researcher can configure a short set of optional questions (an age range, gender identity in your own words, a country) that you can skip; once anyone answers, they lock. Room creators can also invite people by email and open a room on a schedule.

Video game

Research rooms and open play

When you create a room you now choose whether it is for research or for open play. Joining a research room shows a short, plain-language screen explaining that the content you create there may be quoted, always anonymised, in the project's research, and joining is how you agree to that. Open play rooms stay private to the people in them.

Video game

Policy versions, recorded consent

Terms of Service and Privacy Policy now show their version and effective date at the top. When we update them in a way that changes what you agreed to, the next time you sign in you'll see a short re-acceptance screen with links to the updated text. Each acceptance is recorded against your account.

Video game

Live multiplayer presence

The "online" badge next to each room on your dashboard now updates in real time as players come and go, no page refresh needed. The lobby chips and the in-room presence pill also got a tighter visual pass with a "you" marker so it is clear which traveller is yours.

Video game

Right to forget your account

A new Danger zone on your profile lets you delete your account yourself, with a 30-day grace window so you can change your mind. Four reminder emails arrive over the month, each with a one-click cancel link. If you created rooms where other players have written journals, the action is blocked until you handle those rooms first, so collaborators don't lose work. Available in English, French, and Spanish.

Board game

Version 1.0, and a second playtest

The complete first edition came together: 35 map tiles, 81 cards across five colour-coded piles, the rulebook, and a write-in player booklet for each player. The game was played with the EDGES research group at UBC, an academic research-group session that fed straight back into the design.

October 2025
Board game

First public playtest

JOURNEYWAYS took part in ProtoCON BC in Vancouver, a convention for playtesting game prototypes, and met its first table of players. Watching people pick cards, place tiles, and bend the loose rules to their own ends is where the game learns the most.
