# Playtesting and session protocol

This document describes how JOURNEYWAYS sessions are conducted: the facilitator's role, the session structure, recruitment, materials needed, the consent flow at the table, the observation method, and the debrief. It is the session-level counterpart to [COMPLIANCE.md](COMPLIANCE.md) (frameworks), [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md) (participant data), and [DATA_GOVERNANCE.md](DATA_GOVERNANCE.md) (materials custody). The play-side has no direct counterpart because participation in the videogame is a self-service online flow; here, every session is mediated by a human facilitator.

Claims here are anchored to sibling documents, to the rulebook PDF at <https://www.journeyways.ca/download/JOURNEYWAYS%20-%20Rulebook.pdf>, and to the upstream methodology notes at `~/apps/obsidian/Academia/Journeyways/Foundations/proposal-summary.md` ("Methodology"). Items marked **planned** describe the post-BREB research phase (filing planned summer 2026).

## 3. The facilitator

JOURNEYWAYS sessions are facilitated. The facilitator is typically the researcher (Adri M.) and has several roles at once:

- **Host.** Welcomes participants, makes the space feel safe and unhurried.
- **Rules guide.** Introduces the loose-rules philosophy — the rulebook forbids almost nothing, but new players need scaffolding to learn that they have authorship over the rules.
- **Consent steward.** Explains what will and will not be recorded, distributes and collects the photo waiver, and confirms verbal agreement before any photograph is taken.
- **Observer.** Takes written observation notes in real time, referring to participants by in-session pseudonym (first-name initial or participant code).
- **Designer-in-the-room.** Notices what mechanics are working, what players are inventing on their own, what frustrates or surprises. Feeds this back into v2 and the framework.

The facilitator role does **not** include playing a character or directing the narrative. The facilitator may model the loose-rules disposition by participating briefly, but the facilitator is not a player at the table for the duration of the session.

## 4. Session structure

A typical playtest session, ~2.5 to 3 hours including arrival and debrief:

### 4.1 Arrival and informal introduction (15-20 min)

Participants arrive, settle in, look at the materials laid out on the table. The facilitator distributes a printed copy of the photo waiver and a short session overview. Informal conversation, low pressure.

### 4.2 Verbal opening (10 min)

The facilitator speaks briefly to the whole table:

- **Purpose**: this session is a **playtest** of the game; it is not (currently) a research-data-collection session. The goal is to refine the game with the players' feedback.
- **What will be recorded**: written observation notes by the facilitator (with the participant referred to by in-session pseudonym), and photographs of the table and the materials in play. Photographs of participants are taken only of those who sign the photo waiver.
- **What will not be recorded**: audio. Video. Any social-media post or public communication that identifies a participant.
- **Loose rules**: the rules in the rulebook are the starting point. Anything can be modified, bent, or broken at any moment, by anyone at the table, with consent of the other players. Rule-breaking is the success metric, not the failure mode.
- **Withdrawal**: any participant can leave the table at any time without explanation or penalty. Their materials will be handled as described in [PRIVACY.md §7.4](PRIVACY.md#74-withdrawal-of-participation).
- **Questions**: the facilitator invites questions before the session begins.

### 4.3 Photo waiver collection (5 min)

The facilitator collects signed photo waivers from those participants who consent to being photographed. Participants who decline are not photographed during the session; they are welcome to participate in the game otherwise.

### 4.4 Character creation (20-30 min)

Each player receives a player booklet, opens to the character-creation page, and works through the Character Origins prompts (my_name, where_from, where_now, what_who, why, goal — see `Foundations/proposal-summary.md`, "Material qualities"). Players may share what they wrote, share part of it, or keep it private. The facilitator does not require sharing.

### 4.5 Play (60-90 min)

The session proceeds per the rulebook. The facilitator observes; the players play. Key moments to note:

- **Pile choice and card pick.** Players choose which colour pile to pick from and pick blind. Agency at the level of category, discovery at the level of card. The verb is **pick**, never "draw" — in this game "draw" refers to illustrating on a tile, in a booklet, or on a sticky note. The facilitator's vocabulary models this distinction.
- **Tile placement.** Players place tiles in arrangement on the table; the canonical starting point is the **Start** tile.
- **Player-authored mechanics.** When players propose a rule modification or invent a new mechanic mid-game, the facilitator notes the modification, the moment it emerged, and what the table decided. Player-authored modifications that work well at multiple sessions are candidates for incorporation into v2.
- **Default end condition**: 5 Black (Countdown) cards picked. The session can end earlier or later by table consensus.

### 4.6 Closing debrief (15-25 min)

After the play ends, the facilitator opens a short debrief:

- **Open question**: what did each player notice?
- **Open question**: what felt good? what got in the way? what would you change?
- **Specific question**: what rule did you wish you could have broken? what rule did you actually break? would you want that to become a canonical option?
- **Looking-ahead question** (when relevant): would you be interested in joining a research session once the formal research phase opens? (No commitment is requested; the question gauges interest only.)

The facilitator captures the debrief in written notes. **No audio recording** of the debrief in the playtesting phase.

## 5. Materials per session

A standard playtest session needs the following materials prepared in advance.

### 5.1 The game itself

- One complete copy of v1.0: 35 map tiles, 81 cards across five piles, one player booklet per expected player, one rulebook.
- An additional small set of blank tiles and blank cards for player-authored content (when players invent a tile or a card on the fly, blanks let them physicalise it immediately).

### 5.2 Facilitator's kit

- Printed copies of the **photo waiver** (one per expected participant, plus spares).
- A pen for signing.
- A notebook and pen for the facilitator's written observation notes.
- A camera or phone for waivered photography, with the camera-roll cloud sync **disabled** before the session (see [DATA_GOVERNANCE.md §5.1](DATA_GOVERNANCE.md#51-researcher-device-hygiene)).
- A session-overview handout (short, one page; lays out the session structure and the consent posture).
- (Optional) snacks and water — these help the unhurried pace.

### 5.3 Venue requirements

- A quiet, low-stimulation room.
- A table large enough for the tile arrangement plus participant booklets (35 tiles can sprawl; a 1.5 m × 1.5 m table or larger is comfortable).
- Predictable lighting (no flicker; daylight or steady warm artificial light).
- Seating for the expected number of players plus the facilitator.
- Accessibility: the facilitator confirms wheelchair access, step-free entry, and any participant-specific accommodations before the session. See [COMPLIANCE.md §5.3](COMPLIANCE.md#53-physical-accessibility).

## 6. Research-phase additions (planned)

Activates after UBC BREB approval (filing planned summer 2026). The protocol changes in the following ways:

### 6.1 Recruitment

Research-phase recruitment is documented in the BREB submission and may involve: a recruitment flyer or call posted through approved channels, a sign-up form that collects contact details and any inclusion-criteria information, and a confirmation process before a session is scheduled. Inclusion criteria are study-specific (e.g. adult age range, community of interest, language match) and are documented per study.

### 6.2 Informed consent

The verbal opening is replaced by (or rather, supplemented by) a **written informed consent** flow. The consent form is the one approved by UBC BREB; it states the study's purpose, the procedures, the risks and benefits, the data-handling commitments, the participant's rights (including the right to withdraw and the withdrawal window), and the researcher's contact. Where possible, the form is shared with participants in advance so they can read it before the session.

The form is read aloud at the session opening (or made available in writing for participants who prefer to read), questions are answered, and every participant signs before any data-generating activity begins.

### 6.3 Combined photo-and-audio waiver

Replaces the standalone photo waiver. Specifies recording devices, storage and retention, access controls, and the participant's rights. See [COMPLIANCE.md §3.3](COMPLIANCE.md#33-audio-waiver-planned).

### 6.4 Audio recording

Audio recording begins. The facilitator uses a dedicated recorder. Files are transferred to encrypted storage immediately after the session per [DATA_GOVERNANCE.md §4.2](DATA_GOVERNANCE.md#42-research-phase-data-classes-planned).

### 6.5 Retained booklets

Completed player booklets are **retained by the researcher** at the end of the session. The informed consent form states this explicitly, so participants know in advance that their booklet becomes part of the research record and they will not take it home. Each booklet is cataloged by participant pseudonym.

### 6.6 Demographic intake

Where a specific study calls for demographic information (age range, country, gender identity, community of interest), a short per-session intake form is administered before the session begins. Participants may decline any question; declining a required question means not participating in that specific study. Information is bound to the session, not added to a standing profile. See [PRIVACY.md §3.6](PRIVACY.md#36-research-phase-additions-planned).

### 6.7 Post-session interview

A short semi-structured interview is conducted after the closing debrief, audio-recorded under the combined waiver, and later transcribed and anonymised. The interview is part of the research methodology (see `Foundations/proposal-summary.md`, "Methodology") and gathers the reflective and social dimensions of the gameplay experience that the in-session observation could not capture.

### 6.8 Withdrawal mechanics

Withdrawal-from-the-research-record mechanics are specified on the consent form. A defined window (planned default: two weeks post-session, to be confirmed in the BREB submission) allows a participant to ask for their materials to be anonymised, partially excluded, or destroyed.

## 8. Observation method

Written observation notes are the primary data instrument during playtests. The discipline:

- **In-session pseudonyms.** Notes refer to participants by first-name initial or participant code (e.g. "P1", "P2"). Full real names do not appear in notes.
- **Real-time + post-session.** The facilitator notes salient moments during play (a rule modification, an emotional moment, a creative tile placement, a piece of player feedback). Within 48 hours, the notes are expanded and digitised; gaps are filled in from memory while it is fresh.
- **Three layers** in each session's notes: (a) factual record — what happened, what was picked, what was placed; (b) design observations — what mechanics worked, what didn't, what variations players invented; (c) participant-feedback summary — what each player said in the debrief, attributed by pseudonym.
- **Confidentiality discipline.** Specific quotations are recorded verbatim where useful; quoted content is treated as Tier B (see [DATA_GOVERNANCE.md §2](DATA_GOVERNANCE.md#2-data-classification)).
- **Digitised note format.** Plain-text Markdown files, one per session, in the encrypted session directory; cross-referenced to the photographs taken at the same session.

## 9. Photo discipline

When the photo waiver permits, photographs at sessions follow a discipline:

- **What is photographed**: the table, the tile arrangement, the cards in play, participants engaged in the activity (only of those who signed the waiver).
- **What is not photographed**: identifiable content of a participant's booklet (the booklet is the player's private artefact); identifying details in the room (badges, name placards) unless the participant requested otherwise.
- **Camera-roll discipline**: the device's cloud sync is disabled before the session; photographs are moved to encrypted local storage within 48 hours and deleted from the camera-roll. See [DATA_GOVERNANCE.md §5.1](DATA_GOVERNANCE.md#51-researcher-device-hygiene).
- **Filing**: photographs filed under `sessions/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<venue>/photos/` in the encrypted project directory, cross-referenced to the corresponding observation notes.
